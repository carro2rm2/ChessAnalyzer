    Waiting for stretch comparison.

    Engine-free positional move quality (0–100) compares the position immediately before a move with the position immediately after it, before the opponent replies. It uses material, piece placement, pawn structure, and king exposure. Scores are centered at 50; higher means the move improved the position according to this simple heuristic. Because it stops before the opponent's reply, their subsequent choices do not affect this score. It is not engine accuracy or a best-move comparison, and it can miss tactics and longer-term plans. Move safety is the percentage of moves where the piece just moved was not immediately legally capturable. It is a simple tactical-safety flag. Aggressiveness is a custom heuristic in points per move based on captures, forward/central pawn advances, moves toward the enemy king, and checks that are followed by a capture or checkmate. Check follow-through is the percentage of checks followed by at least a one-point net material gain (a pawn or more) after the opponent has had a chance to reply, within the checking player's next three turns, or checkmate. Material is valued pawn=1, knight/bishop=3, rook=5, queen=9. This is a material-based proxy, not proof that the check was objectively best. Forks, pins, threats, and positional pressure are not fully measured.

    Move-pattern rates are normalized per 20 half-moves. “Exposed” means a moved piece was legally capturable immediately afterward; it is a review flag, not proof of a blunder.

    How to interpret this

    Floor: The 20th percentile of rolling performance ratings. It represents a lower typical stretch, not a guaranteed minimum.

    Typical: The median rolling performance rating.

    Ceiling: The 80th percentile of rolling performance ratings. It represents a stronger typical stretch.

    Performance trend: The average of the five most recent rolling performance ratings minus the average of the first five. A positive number means recent results were stronger than earlier results.

    Best stretch: The strongest individual window found in the games analyzed. A short hot streak is less reliable than a longer sample.

    The trend is a comparison of two periods, not an official Chess.com rating change or a guarantee of future results.
